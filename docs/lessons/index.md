# Lessons

Three lessons, taken in order, move from the principles of open science to managing research data and to using artificial intelligence responsibly. Each in-person lesson is 50 minutes: introduction (5 min), core concepts (25 min), hands-on activity (15 min), and wrap-up (5 min). Lesson 1 also has a self-paced homework page that carries the full material; every lesson can be taken with an [AI tutor](../about/ai-tutor.md).

* [Lesson 1: Foundations of Open Science](01-open-science.md) - A 50-minute in-person lecture: what open science is, its six pillars, the nine Gold Standard Science tenets, and the 2026 public-access and publication-cost rules, with Superfund examples from Arizona and New Mexico.
* [Lesson 1 homework: Open Science, self-paced](01-open-science-self-paced.md) - The self-paced companion to Lesson 1: twelve modules with checkpoints on the six pillars, Gold Standard Science in depth, the 2026 public-access and publication-cost landscape, a full self-assessment, and the complete quiz.
* [Lesson 2: Modern Data Management for Computational Research](02-data-management.md) - The data life cycle, FAIR and CARE principles, the 2026 NIH and NSF data management and sharing plan formats, data rescue, and a two-site metal-mixture DMP exercise.
* [Lesson 3: Ethics and Artificial Intelligence](03-ai-ethics.md) - Sources of AI bias, responsible and agentic AI use in research, NIH and journal AI rules, energy and water costs, the 2026 regulatory landscape, and six discussion scenarios.
